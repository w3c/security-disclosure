This document defines how to report suspected security vulnerabilities in W3C standards and specifications (technical reports), so that issues can be triaged, confirmed, and resolved through the appropriate W3C processes.
It is not for reporting vulnerabilities in software implementations or W3C operational infrastructure (see Out of scope).

This document is a work in progress and may be changed at any time and without notice.

Introduction

Overview

W3C standards and specifications can contain design issues that create security risks for users, implementers, or the Web platform. Reports from researchers and the wider community help W3C identify those issues and route them to the people who can assess and address them.

Purpose

This policy describes how W3C receives, triages, routes, and coordinates reports about suspected vulnerabilities in W3C standards and specifications.

Scope and Application

W3C is a standards development organization that publishes Technical Reports, including W3C Recommendations, Notes, and Registries, which describe Web technologies and specifications.

This policy applies to design vulnerabilities or security issues described in W3C specifications.

This policy does NOT cover:

  • Implementation, tooling, or configuration vulnerabilities in products, open-source projects, or services that may implement W3C standards. These issues should be reported to the corresponding vendor or maintainer.
  • Vulnerabilities in W3C infrastructure and services that support w3.org domains. These are the responsibility of W3C Systems, which has its vulnerability disclosure policy.

Process and Policy

W3C's response depends on the type of technical report in which the issue was found, the severity of the issue, and the complexity of the response. W3C will work to resolve confirmed security issues without unnecessary delay. This policy was informed by [[?ISO-IEC-29147]] and [[?ISO-IEC-30111]].

W3C handles and routes reports according to the type and maturity of the document in which the security issue is reported.

Reporting a Vulnerability in a W3C Standard

If you think you have found a vulnerability in a W3C document, please contact standards-vulnerability@w3.org for coordinated disclosure.

This email serves as the point of reference for the initial triage by the W3C Team. After triage, the W3C Team will involve the appropriate group depending on the report and the affected specification. This normally includes the Team contact, chair, and editors, and may include other people when their input is needed.

It is advisable to send encrypted messages using the PGP key to standards-vulnerability@w3.org. This email does not have a public archive.

The reporting email address and PGP key will be made available before publication. In the meantime, please contact simone@w3.org.

To facilitate the process, your advisory should include details such as those recommended by [[?ISO-IEC-29147]] and [[?NIST-SP-800-216]]:

  • The specific document in which the vulnerability is present (e.g., its title, stable URL, and publication date).
  • A concise description of the type of vulnerability (e.g., Use of unsafe cryptographic algorithms).
  • Steps to reproduce the vulnerability, ideally a benign, non-destructive proof of concept.
  • The impact of exploiting this vulnerability and how it could be used in an attack scenario.
  • Proposed corrective or mitigating measures, if any.
  • Your contact details (optional; a working return channel is needed for acknowledgments and status updates, but the reporter does not need to disclose their identity).

Please note that:

  • No "Bug Bounties": W3C does not pay "bug bounties" for reported vulnerabilities.
  • Confidentiality: Non-public vulnerability information is handled through restricted channels and shared only as needed to assess or coordinate the report. Reporters are asked to use the agreed restricted channel while confidential coordination continues. The coordinator will tell participants when the matter can move to the appropriate public process.
  • Researcher Protections: W3C does not intend to initiate legal action against a reporter solely for submitting a good-faith report under this policy. This policy does not authorize testing of systems, services, or products controlled by third parties, or conduct inconsistent with applicable law.

Receipt and Acknowledgment of the Security Issue (VD)

W3C aims to acknowledge security issues within 3 business days and establish a communication channel with the reporter. The W3C Team will designate a coordinator for the report.

Verification of the Security Issues (VH)

After the acknowledgement, the security issue will be evaluated to determine its validity, technical severity, relevance, and potential impact.

W3C aims to provide the reporter with an initial assessment within 15 business days. If evaluation is not complete by then, W3C will instead provide a status update and the next review date. If more information is needed, W3C will request additional details from the reporter.

The W3C Team is responsible for tracking these response targets and for escalating the report when more time, coordination, or authority is needed.

Post-Verification Lifecycle and Reporter Updates (VH/VD)

After a security issue is confirmed, the coordinator will use an appropriate restricted coordination channel or tracking mechanism to record the current phase, the next review point, and the W3C Group or other party currently responsible for the response for each confirmed affected document that W3C is coordinating. A repository security advisory, when used, may serve as both the record and the private discussion channel; a separate record is not required.

No fixed state model is required. The record may describe response planning or work, multi-party coordination, coordinated disclosure, follow-up, or closure. These descriptions are not a required linear sequence, and more than one may apply.

When a working return channel is available, the coordinator will tell the reporter the current phase, who is currently coordinating the response, whether further input is needed, and either the next review date or an update cadence appropriate to the case. While W3C continues to coordinate the case, the coordinator will provide status updates at those points through the agreed channel. Review timing is set case by case, taking account of severity, complexity, and the number of parties involved. A short no-change message, including one sent through an existing repository security advisory, is sufficient and need not disclose sensitive implementation details.

Before closing a case, the coordinator will record an outcome for each confirmed affected document that W3C is coordinating. An outcome may be a document update, an advisory or mitigation, transfer to another responsible process or coordinator, or closure without a change with rationale. Completing work on the originally reported document does not close the case while another confirmed affected document that W3C is coordinating lacks a recorded outcome. These requirements establish review and update points rather than a universal remediation deadline.

Handling and Resolution Development (VH)

The coordinator will then work with the groups responsible for the affected document and, when a working return channel is available, the reporter. In most cases, resolution will primarily involve updating the relevant document.

During non-public coordination, the coordinator will use an appropriate restricted channel. After disclosure, follow-up may move to the usual public issue or mailing-list process of the responsible group or other party.

W3C Recommendations - Published

These are completed, community-reviewed standards.

  • If the Working Group that produced the Recommendation is still active, the report is routed to that Working Group to determine the most suitable approach. The vulnerability might be addressed via:

    • An erratum (for minor issues or corrections that do not change the normative content). W3C Working Groups must keep a public record of errors reported for Recommendations. Such error reports should be compiled no less frequently than quarterly.
    • An updated Recommendation specification document (revising the Recommendation).
    • An entirely new document to handle the issue.

  • If the Working Group is closed, possible responses include the maintenance-without-a-group mechanisms provided by the W3C Process. The severity of the issue informs the available response:

    • Minor issues may be covered with errata (potentially maintained by the W3C Team if no group is chartered).
    • For more significant updates, the W3C Team may consider a different approach.

Class 3 or 4 changes, as per W3C Process, can trigger more extensive review and patent policy implications.

Candidate Recommendations (CR) and Working Drafts (WD) - In Development

These are documents adopted by a W3C Working Group, but that they are not yet finalized.

  • W3C Working Groups should formally address any substantive review comments about a technical report in a timely manner.

Editor's Drafts - Early Stage/Individual Contributions

These are not officially adopted documents in W3C. Editor's Drafts, that haven't been published as a W3C specification, have no official standing.

  • The issue should be handled and discussed with the editors of the document.

Discontinued, Obsolete, or Rescinded Documents

  • W3C Technical Reports that have been marked as discontinued, obsolete, or rescinded are unlikely to have action taken on them in response to a security issue report. Still, the Team should evaluate listing them in the Status of the document (SOTD).

Community Group Reports

These are not officially adopted documents in W3C, and the Community and Business Group Process regulates them.

  • Any issues found should be handled and discussed with the editors of the document.

Member’s Submissions

These are not officially adopted documents in W3C. Member Submissions are proposed by Members for consideration.

  • Any issues found should be handled and discussed with the editors of the document.

The issue will be handled according to the member's policy.

Release and Dissemination (VD)

The coordinator will work with the reporter and affected parties to record a disclosure plan. If public disclosure is planned, the plan will include a target date once sufficient information is available, taking into account the severity of the issue, available mitigations, the maturity of the affected document, and any multi-party coordination. The plan may be revised through the review points described above if early disclosure would materially increase risk. Public disclosure does not require every related document or implementation update to be complete.

Public disclosure may include a description of the vulnerability, information that allows users to identify the affected standard, the impact and severity, and available information to help implementers and users mitigate or remediate the issue. Where W3C publishes an advisory, it will normally acknowledge the reporter if public credit is requested.

Published repository security advisories are listed in the public security advisory index for W3C group repositories.
